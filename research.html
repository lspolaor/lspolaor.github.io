Research & Publications

My research is in the calculus of variations and geometric analysis. I study the regularity of solutions to geometric variational problems, such as minimal surfaces and area-minimizing currents, free boundary problems like the obstacle and Bernoulli problems, and multivalued harmonic functions, using tools from geometric measure theory and partial differential equations. A recurring theme is the structure of singularities: what a solution looks like near a singular point, whether its blow-up is unique, and how large and how regular the singular set can be.

Publications and preprints

  1. On cylindrical singularities of minimal hypersurfaces

    with A. Halavati · preprint (2026) · arXiv:2610.10754

    Summary

    Simon's theorem describes a minimal hypersurface near a cylindrical singularity, a point where it looks like a cone times a line, provided the nearby singular set has no holes. We construct, in ℝ9 with a smooth conformally flat metric, a minimal hypersurface whose tangent cone at the origin is C3,3 × ℝ and which has a sequence of isolated singular points, modelled on C4,3, accumulating there. So the no-holes condition can genuinely fail; whether this can happen for the Euclidean metric is open.

  2. Stationary varifolds with singularities II

    with C. De Lellis, J. Hirsch and Z. Lihn · preprint (2026) · arXiv:2609.20647

    Summary

    For every dimension m ≥ 3 we construct, for a smooth Riemannian metric, a stationary integral m-varifold with a flat tangent plane of multiplicity two at an interior point p, which is a smooth immersed minimal surface away from p but has infinite topology in every neighborhood of p. Unlike the construction in our earlier paper, the metric here is smooth.

  3. Fine structure of the two-phase Bernoulli free boundaries in 2d

    with L. Ferreri and B. Velichkov · preprint (2026) · arXiv:2604.23843

    Summary

    In the two-phase Bernoulli problem the positive and the negative phase can stick together along part of the free boundary and separate elsewhere; the points where they separate are branch points. In dimension two we describe the fine structure of the free boundary near these branch points.

  4. Stationary and stable varifolds with singularities

    with C. De Lellis and J. Hirsch · preprint (2025) · arXiv:2509.21508

    Summary

    Can a stationary surface be singular at a point where it looks like a flat plane counted twice? We construct minimal hypersurfaces in ℝm+1, for a C1,α metric, made of two sheets with a sequence of catenoidal necks, or of floating disks, accumulating at an isolated flat singular point of multiplicity two.

  5. Measure 0 of the singular set for 2-valued stationary hypercurrents

    with J. Hirsch · preprint (2025) · arXiv:2509.05544

    Summary

    Stationary surfaces, unlike area-minimizing ones, can a priori be singular on large sets, and points where two sheets come together are the first obstruction to a regularity theory. We prove that the singular set of a two-valued stationary hypercurrent has measure zero.

  6. Carleman estimates for stationary Q-valued maps: a variational approach

    with A. Halavati · preprint (2025) · arXiv:2508.14388

    Summary

    Carleman estimates are a classical route to unique continuation. We prove Carleman estimates for multivalued maps that are only stationary for the Dirichlet energy, a setting where no equation is available in the usual sense, with a purely variational argument.

  7. Structure of two-dimensional mod (q) area-minimizing currents near flat singularities: the codimension one case

    with A. Skorobogatova and S. Stuvard · J. Math. Pures Appl. (2026) · arXiv:2506.17813

    Summary

    Surfaces that minimize area modulo q can be singular even where they look flat: several sheets may come together along a plane counted with multiplicity. For two-dimensional surfaces in three-dimensional space we describe the structure of a mod q minimizer near such flat singular points.

  8. Rectifiability of the singular set and uniqueness of tangent cones for semicalibrated currents

    with P. Minter, D. Parise and A. Skorobogatova · preprint (2024) · arXiv:2409.03037

    Summary

    Semicalibrated currents, such as special Legendrian cycles and pseudoholomorphic curves, are only almost minimizing. We show that their interior singular set is (m−2)-rectifiable and that the tangent cone is unique at almost every singular point, extending recent results for area-minimizing currents.

  9. Unique continuation for nonlinear variational problems

    with L. Ferreri and B. Velichkov · preprint (2024) · arXiv:2408.00405

    Summary

    Unique continuation asks whether a solution is determined by its behavior on an arbitrarily small open set. We prove unique continuation results for a class of nonlinear variational problems, beyond the linear setting where the classical tools apply.

  10. On the boundary branching set of the one-phase problem

    with L. Ferreri and B. Velichkov · preprint (2024) · arXiv:2407.15230

    Summary

    When the one-phase Bernoulli problem is posed inside a container, the free boundary can stick to the wall on some regions and leave it on others. We study the boundary branching set, made of the points where the free boundary detaches from the fixed boundary.

  11. On the fine structure of the solutions to nonlinear thin two-membrane problems in 2D

    with L. Ferreri and B. Velichkov · Rev. Mat. Iberoam. (2024) · arXiv:2405.05799

    Summary

    Two-membrane problems model two elastic membranes that cannot cross and may stick together on an unknown region. For nonlinear thin two-membrane problems in dimension two we describe the fine structure of the solutions near the points where the membranes separate.

  12. Dimension of the singular set for 2-valued stationary Lipschitz graphs

    with J. Hirsch · Duke Math. J. (2025) · arXiv:2401.00279

    Summary

    A minimal hypersurface that is a two-valued Lipschitz graph can branch: its two sheets meet and separate along a singular set. For graphs that are only stationary, not minimizing, we bound the Hausdorff dimension of this singular set.

  13. Excess decay for minimizing hypercurrents mod 2Q

    with C. De Lellis, J. Hirsch, A. Marchese and S. Stuvard · Nonlinear Anal. 247 (2024), 113606 · arXiv:2308.08704

    Summary

    For hypersurfaces minimizing area modulo an even integer 2Q, a plane counted Q times is a singular configuration. We prove an excess decay theorem near such flat configurations: if the surface is close to a plane of multiplicity Q at one scale, it is quantitatively closer at smaller scales.

  14. The symmetric (log-)epiperimetric inequality and a decay-growth estimate

    with N. Edelen and B. Velichkov · Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations (2024) · arXiv:2304.11129

    Summary

    Epiperimetric inequalities control how fast a solution approaches its blow-up as the scale decreases. We prove a symmetric version, which also controls what happens as the scale increases, and deduce a decay-growth estimate for the distance from a cone across scales.

  15. Interior regularity for two-dimensional stationary Q-valued maps

    with J. Hirsch · Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. (2024) · arXiv:2211.09052

    Summary

    Almgren's multivalued functions describe minimal surfaces near points of higher multiplicity. Minimizers of the Dirichlet energy are well understood, while maps that are only stationary are much harder to handle. In dimension two we prove interior regularity for stationary Q-valued maps.

  16. A strong maximum principle for minimizers of the one-phase Bernoulli problem

    with N. Edelen and B. Velichkov · Indiana Univ. Math. J. (2023) · arXiv:2205.00401

    Summary

    We prove a strong maximum principle for the one-phase Bernoulli problem: if two ordered minimizers have free boundaries that touch, then they coincide, even when the contact happens at a singular point.

  17. Fine structure of the singular set of area minimizing hypersurfaces modulo p

    with C. De Lellis, J. Hirsch, A. Marchese and S. Stuvard · Acta Math. (2026) · arXiv:2201.10204

    Summary

    We complete the description of hypersurfaces minimizing area modulo p, for every p: up to a closed set of codimension two, the singular set is a C1,α submanifold along which finitely many smooth sheets meet, with multiplicities adding up to p. The key new step shows that singular points with flat blow-ups, which occur for even p, form a set of codimension at least two.

  18. (Quasi-)conformal methods in two-dimensional free boundary problems

    with G. De Philippis and B. Velichkov · J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2024) · arXiv:2110.14075

    Summary

    In the plane, complex analysis provides tools with no analogue in higher dimensions. We develop conformal and quasi-conformal methods for free boundary problems in dimension two and use them to study the free boundary of two-phase problems near branch points.

  19. Rectifiability and almost everywhere uniqueness of the blow-up for the vectorial Bernoulli free boundaries

    with G. De Philippis, M. Engelstein and B. Velichkov · preprint (2021) · arXiv:2107.12485

    Summary

    For the vectorial Bernoulli problem without sign constraints, the free boundary contains branching points, where the blow-up is a linear map. We show that these points form rectifiable strata of the expected dimensions and that the blow-up is unique at almost every branching point.

  20. Area minimizing hypersurfaces modulo p: a geometric free-boundary problem

    with C. De Lellis, J. Hirsch, A. Marchese and S. Stuvard · J. Funct. Anal. 290 (2026), Paper No. 111442 · arXiv:2105.08135 · journal

    Summary

    A hypersurface minimizing area modulo p can be singular in codimension one, where several smooth sheets meet along a common edge, as in soap films. For odd p we show that, outside a closed set of codimension two, a minimizer consists of finitely many smooth minimal sheets meeting along a common C1,α boundary, a free boundary of geometric nature. For even p the same holds near every point where one tangent cone has this form.

  21. Quantitative stability for minimizing Yamabe metrics

    with M. Engelstein and R. Neumayer · Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. (2022) · arXiv:2009.14362

    Summary

    The Yamabe problem asks for a metric of constant scalar curvature in a given conformal class. We prove that a conformal metric whose Yamabe energy is almost minimal is quantitatively close to a minimizing Yamabe metric, and we study the optimal rate: quadratic in non-degenerate situations, possibly worse in degenerate ones.

  22. Singular behavior and generic regularity of min-max minimal hypersurfaces

    with O. Chodosh and Y. Liokumovich · Ars Inveniendi Analytica (2022), Paper No. 2 · arXiv:2007.11560

    Summary

    Min-max methods produce minimal hypersurfaces in every closed manifold, but in dimension eight these may have isolated singular points. We analyze the singularities of one-parameter min-max hypersurfaces and show, for instance, that a generic eight-dimensional manifold with positive Ricci curvature contains a smooth minimal hypersurface.

  23. On the logarithmic epiperimetric inequality for the obstacle problem

    with B. Velichkov · Math. Eng. (2021) · arXiv:2007.02346

    Summary

    We revisit the logarithmic epiperimetric inequality at the singular points of the obstacle problem and its consequences: uniqueness of blow-ups with an explicit rate and regularity of the singular set.

  24. Regularity of the free boundary for the two-phase Bernoulli problem

    with G. De Philippis and B. Velichkov · Invent. Math. 225 (2021), 347–394 · arXiv:1911.02165

    Summary

    In the two-phase Bernoulli problem the positive and the negative phase may stick together on part of the free boundary and separate elsewhere. We prove that, in every dimension, both free boundaries are C1,η graphs near every two-phase point, including the branch points where the phases separate. This completes the regularity theory initiated by Alt, Caffarelli and Friedman.

  25. Regularity of minimal surfaces near quadratic cones

    with N. Edelen · Ann. of Math. 198 (2023), 1013–1046 · arXiv:1910.00441 · journal

    Summary

    The Simons cone is the simplest singular area-minimizing hypersurface. We show that every minimal surface close enough to a minimizing quadratic cone is a smooth perturbation either of the cone itself or of one of the smooth leaves of its Hardt–Simon foliation, so these singularities determine the structure of all nearby minimal surfaces.

  26. Almost everywhere uniqueness of blow-up limits for the lower dimensional obstacle problem

    with M. Colombo and B. Velichkov · Interfaces Free Bound. (2021) · arXiv:1908.01413

    Summary

    For the thin obstacle (Signorini) problem we prove that the blow-up limit is unique at almost every point of the free boundary.

  27. The Riemannian quantitative isoperimetric inequality

    with O. Chodosh and M. Engelstein · J. Eur. Math. Soc. (2022) · arXiv:1908.00677

    Summary

    The quantitative isoperimetric inequality of Fusco, Maggi and Pratelli says that a set in ℝn whose perimeter is almost minimal for its volume is close to a ball. We prove the analogous statement on closed Riemannian manifolds, where the role of the ball is played by isoperimetric regions.

  28. Free boundary regularity for a multiphase shape optimization problem

    with B. Trey and B. Velichkov · Comm. Partial Differential Equations 45 (2020), 77–108 · arXiv:1810.06963

    Summary

    We study configurations of disjoint cells in a box that minimize the sum of their first Dirichlet eigenvalues and their volumes. In the plane we prove that the optimal cells are regular everywhere, including where two cells touch and where they meet the walls of the box.

  29. On the asymptotic behavior of the solutions to parabolic variational inequalities

    with M. Colombo and B. Velichkov · J. Reine Angew. Math. 768 (2020), 149–182 · arXiv:1809.06075

    Summary

    We prove a Łojasiewicz inequality for energies constrained to a convex set, where Simon's classical argument does not apply, and use it to show that solutions of the parabolic obstacle and thin obstacle problems converge, as time goes to infinity, to a stationary solution at an explicit rate. The same inequality gives a constructive proof of logarithmic epiperimetric inequalities.

  30. (Log-)epiperimetric inequality and regularity over smooth cones for almost area-minimizing currents

    with M. Engelstein and B. Velichkov · Geom. Topol. 23 (2019), 513–540 · arXiv:1802.00418 · journal

    Summary

    We prove (logarithmic) epiperimetric inequalities near area-minimizing cones with smooth cross-section. As a consequence, an almost area-minimizing current that has such a cone as one of its tangent cones has it as its unique tangent cone, and near the vertex it is a C1,α (or C1,log) graph over the cone. This gives a direct variational route to theorems of Allard–Almgren and Simon.

  31. Uniqueness of the blow-up at isolated singularities for the Alt–Caffarelli functional

    with M. Engelstein and B. Velichkov · Duke Math. J. 169 (2020), 1541–1601 · arXiv:1801.09276 · journal

    Summary

    At a singular point of a one-phase free boundary whose blow-up is a cone with an isolated singularity, we prove that the blow-up is unique and that nearby the free boundary is a C1,log graph over the cone, or C1,α when the cone is integrable. The tool is a logarithmic epiperimetric inequality for the Weiss energy.

  32. The singular set of minimal surfaces near polyhedral cones

    with M. Colombo and N. Edelen · J. Differential Geom. 120 (2022), 411–503 · arXiv:1709.09957 · journal

    Summary

    Soap films meet in threes along curves at 120° and in fours at isolated points, as described by Plateau's laws. We prove the analogous structure in every dimension for minimizing clusters and size-minimizing currents, through a regularity theorem for stationary varifolds near polyhedral cones.

  33. Direct epiperimetric inequalities for the thin obstacle problem and applications

    with M. Colombo and B. Velichkov · Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 73 (2020), 384–420 · arXiv:1709.03120

    Summary

    For the thin obstacle (Signorini) problem we prove epiperimetric inequalities by explicit constructions: at regular points in every dimension, and in logarithmic form at singular points. Consequences include frequency gaps, the regularity of the regular set, and the covering of each singular stratum by countably many C1,log submanifolds.

  34. A logarithmic epiperimetric inequality for the obstacle problem

    with M. Colombo and B. Velichkov · Geom. Funct. Anal. 28 (2018), 1029–1061 · arXiv:1708.02045

    Summary

    We introduce a logarithmic epiperimetric inequality and prove it at the singular points of the obstacle problem in every dimension. It gives uniqueness of blow-ups with an explicit logarithmic rate and shows that the singular set is covered by countably many C1,log submanifolds (C1,α in the plane).

  35. An epiperimetric inequality for the regularity of some free boundary problems: the 2-dimensional case

    with B. Velichkov · Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 72 (2019), 375–421 · arXiv:1612.01623

    Summary

    We prove epiperimetric inequalities for the one-phase, two-phase and vectorial Bernoulli problems in the plane by constructing explicit competitors. As a consequence, blow-ups are unique, with a rate, at every free boundary point, and the free boundaries are C1,α, two-phase points included.

  36. Quantitative estimate on singularities in isoperimetric clusters

    with M. Colombo · Comm. Anal. Geom. (2019) · arXiv:1609.08597

    Summary

    Minimizing clusters, configurations of regions with prescribed volumes and least total interface area such as soap bubbles stuck together, are singular where several interfaces meet. We prove a quantitative estimate on these singularities.

  37. On the number of singular points for planar multivalued harmonic functions

    with F. Ghiraldin · Manuscripta Math. (2017) · arXiv:1601.01967

    Summary

    Multivalued harmonic functions in the plane, that is, Almgren's Dirichlet-minimizing Q-valued functions, have isolated singular points. We give a quantitative bound on their number in terms of the frequency function.

  38. Almgren's type regularity for semicalibrated currents

    Adv. Math. 350 (2019), 747–815 · arXiv:1511.07705 · journal

    Summary

    Semicalibrated currents, which include special Legendrian cycles and pseudoholomorphic curves, are only almost minimizing. I carry out Almgren's regularity program (Lipschitz approximation, center manifold and blow-up) for this class and show that the interior singular set has dimension at most m−2, as for area-minimizing currents.

  39. Regularity theory for 2-dimensional almost minimal currents III: blowup

    with C. De Lellis and E. Spadaro · J. Differential Geom. 116 (2020), 125–185 · arXiv:1508.05510

    Summary

    Third part of the series: the blow-up analysis. Together with parts I and II it shows that two-dimensional semicalibrated currents, and the spherical cross-sections of three-dimensional area-minimizing cones, have isolated singularities, near which they are finite unions of branched disks.

  40. Regularity theory for 2-dimensional almost minimal currents II: branched center manifold

    with C. De Lellis and E. Spadaro · Ann. PDE 3 (2017), Art. 18 · arXiv:1508.05509

    Summary

    Second part of the series: the construction of a branched center manifold, a branched analogue of Almgren's center manifold adapted to a singular point of multiplicity Q, around which the current is approximated.

  41. Regularity theory for 2-dimensional almost minimizing currents I: Lipschitz approximation

    with C. De Lellis and E. Spadaro · Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 370 (2018), 1783–1801 · arXiv:1508.05507 · journal

    Summary

    First part of a series establishing the regularity theory of two-dimensional almost minimal currents: we approximate the current by the graph of a Lipschitz multivalued function, with errors adapted to the almost-minimality.

  42. Uniqueness of tangent cones for 2-dimensional almost minimizing currents

    with C. De Lellis and E. Spadaro · Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 70 (2017), 1402–1421 · arXiv:1508.05266 · journal

    Summary

    We prove that a two-dimensional almost area-minimizing current has a unique tangent cone at every point, with a polynomial rate of convergence, via an epiperimetric inequality for two-dimensional cones. This covers semicalibrated currents and the spherical cross-sections of three-dimensional area-minimizing cones, and is the starting point of the regularity series in three parts.
